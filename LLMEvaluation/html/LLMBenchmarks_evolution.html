LLM Knowledge Benchmarks

From broad academic knowledge with MMLU to difficult expert reasoning, factuality, calibration, and frontier-level evaluation with GPQA, SimpleQA, MMLU-Pro, and Humanity's Last Exam.

1. Introduction & Recap of Model Evaluation

LLM evaluation is the process of systematically measuring how well a language model performs against a defined capability, task, or quality criterion. Instead of asking only whether a model produces fluent text, evaluation asks: What can the model actually do, how reliably can it do it, and where does it fail?

Important distinction: A benchmark is not simply a collection of questions. It is an evaluation instrument with a defined dataset, task format, scoring methodology, and interpretation of the resulting score.

The benchmark discussion in this lesson moves toward model-level evaluation. The focus shifts from evaluating a complete application such as an LLM-powered classifier or RAG system to evaluating the underlying capabilities of the model itself.

2. The Core LLM Capabilities

A useful way to understand LLM benchmarks is to first decompose the overall capability of an LLM into smaller dimensions.

Knowledge

Can the model retrieve or demonstrate information learned during training?

Reasoning

Can it combine information and derive an answer rather than merely recall one?

Truthfulness

Does the model distinguish factual information from plausible but incorrect claims?

Instruction Following

Can the model follow the requested task, constraints, format, and behavior?

Language

Can it understand and generate language across domains and languages?

Multimodality

Can it reason over inputs such as images, diagrams, or other modalities?

Calibration

Does the model's confidence correspond reasonably well to correctness?

Robustness

Does performance remain reliable when questions become unfamiliar or adversarial?

The key lesson is that “model quality” is multidimensional. A single benchmark score cannot completely describe an LLM.

3. What Is Knowledge Capability?

Parametric Memory

An LLM learns statistical representations of information during pretraining. This information is often referred to as parametric knowledge because it is encoded implicitly in the model parameters rather than retrieved from an external database at inference time.

Knowledge benchmarks attempt to measure whether that learned knowledge can be successfully accessed and used when answering questions.

4. MMLU — Measuring Broad Knowledge

MMLU (Massive Multitask Language Understanding) became an important benchmark for measuring broad academic and professional knowledge. The supplied course outline specifically introduces MMLU as a benchmark covering 57 subjects.

The central idea is simple: instead of evaluating a model on one narrow topic, test it across many different disciplines.

What MMLU Attempts to Measure

  • Broad factual and academic knowledge
  • Performance across many subject areas
  • Multiple-choice question answering
  • Ability to transfer knowledge across domains

Why Multiple Subjects Matter

A model can perform extremely well on programming questions while being weak in law, medicine, history, or philosophy. A broad benchmark exposes this cross-domain variation.

Evaluation Approach What It Reveals
Single-domain benchmark Depth in a particular capability
Multi-domain benchmark Breadth of knowledge
Multiple-choice evaluation Controlled answer space and straightforward scoring
Open-ended evaluation More realistic generation, but harder scoring
Benchmark saturation: As increasingly capable models perform very well on an older benchmark, the benchmark may become less useful for distinguishing newer models. This motivates the development of harder or better-designed evaluations.

5. TruthfulQA — Testing Reliability and Truthfulness

The next branch in the benchmark progression is TruthfulQA. The course outline explicitly frames this branch around testing reliability and later discusses misconceptions and possible contamination introduced during alignment stages.

Why Knowledge Alone Is Not Enough

A model may have access to enormous amounts of information and still generate an answer that sounds convincing but is false.

Truthfulness therefore asks a different question:

Can the model resist producing commonly repeated misconceptions or plausible falsehoods when answering questions?

Knowledge

Does the model know information?

Truthfulness

Does the model state what is actually true?

Reliability

Does the behavior remain dependable across problematic questions?

Key idea: A benchmark can intentionally probe the gap between “what a model can say” and “what a model should say because it is true.”

6. AGI Eval — Human Exam Baselines

The course outline introduces AGI Eval as another branch, specifically connecting it with human exam baselines and bilingual exams.

The important evaluation concept here is comparison against exams or question formats designed for human learners. This creates an intuitive reference point: instead of asking only whether an LLM scores highly, we can ask how its performance compares with human-level examination performance.

Why Human Baselines Matter

A raw score is difficult to interpret without context. Consider: 

Model A: 82%
Model B: 78%

The numbers alone do not tell us whether 82% represents excellent, human-comparable performance or a mediocre result on an easy benchmark.

A benchmark with meaningful human baselines provides additional interpretive context.

7. GPQA — Depth of Knowledge

The course outline introduces GPQA as a benchmark for depth of knowledge and later expands it as “Google-Proof Q&A” with a Diamond dataset.

Why “Google-Proof” Questions?

A useful difficult benchmark should ideally distinguish genuine reasoning and expert knowledge from superficial retrieval or memorization.

GPQA therefore targets difficult questions in which simply recognizing a familiar phrase is insufficient.

8. MMLU-Pro — Fixing Weaknesses in Traditional MMLU

The course outline describes MMLU-Pro as an upgrade involving 10 answer options, 14 disciplines, and a stronger emphasis on reasoning.

Why Create MMLU-Pro?

When a benchmark becomes too familiar or too easy for modern models, researchers need to make the evaluation more discriminative.

The progression can be understood as: 

Older benchmark
      ↓
Models improve
      ↓
Scores become compressed
      ↓
Benchmark becomes less discriminative
      ↓
Harder / better benchmark
      ↓
MMLU-Pro

Reasoning Bias

The deeper purpose is not merely adding more answer choices. A stronger benchmark should make shallow pattern matching less effective and require the model to reason about the underlying problem.

Interview perspective: When asked why newer benchmarks are necessary, answer: “Because benchmark saturation reduces their ability to distinguish between increasingly capable models.”

9. SimpleQA — Open-Ended Short Answers

The course outline introduces SimpleQA as an open-ended short-answer benchmark and specifically calls out calibration metrics.

Why Move Beyond Multiple Choice?

Multiple-choice evaluation provides a constrained answer space: 

A
B
C
D

Open-ended evaluation removes that constraint. The model must actually produce the answer, which introduces additional evaluation challenges:

  • Exact correctness
  • Equivalent wording
  • Partial correctness
  • Abstention behavior
  • Confidence and calibration

Calibration

A well-calibrated model should have confidence that roughly corresponds to the probability that its answer is correct.

Model Behavior Interpretation
High confidence + correct Good confidence
Low confidence + incorrect Reasonable uncertainty
High confidence + incorrect Potentially dangerous overconfidence

10. Humanity's Last Exam — The Modern Frontier

The supplied outline positions Humanity's Last Exam (HLE) as a modern frontier benchmark and connects it with breadth × depth, multimodality, and private evaluation sets.

Breadth × Depth

The fundamental idea is that a strong evaluation should not test only a narrow specialization or only simple factual recall.

Breadth

How many areas of knowledge can the model handle?

Depth

How difficult and specialized are the questions?

Multimodality

Can the model reason using information beyond plain text?

Why Private Evaluation Sets Matter

If benchmark questions become publicly available and are included in model training data, benchmark scores can become contaminated. A model may then appear to perform well partly because it has encountered the evaluation data during training.

Evaluation contamination: A benchmark is most informative when success reflects the capability being measured rather than memorization of the test set.

13. Deep Dive — MMLU Architecture, Log-Likelihoods & Saturated Limits

The outline specifically identifies three deeper MMLU topics: architecture, log-likelihoods, and saturated limits.

Log-Likelihood Based Evaluation

For multiple-choice evaluation, one possible approach is to compare the model's likelihood of candidate answers rather than relying only on generated text. 

Question:
What is 2 + 2?

A. 3
B. 4
C. 5
D. 6

Model scores:
P(A) = 0.02
P(B) = 0.94
P(C) = 0.03
P(D) = 0.01

Predicted answer = B

This illustrates the general idea of selecting the answer associated with the highest model likelihood.

Saturation

If successive models all approach the upper end of a benchmark's score range, the benchmark loses discriminatory power. 

Model A   ███████████████░░░  76%
Model B   █████████████████░  85%
Model C   ██████████████████  91%
Model D   ██████████████████  94%

        ↓

Less room to distinguish future models

14. Deep Dive — TruthfulQA, Misconceptions & Contamination

The instructor-outline explicitly highlights misconceptions and alignment-stage contamination as part of this deep dive.

Why Misconceptions Are Important

The web contains huge amounts of repeated information. Repetition does not automatically make information true.

An LLM trained to model language distributions can therefore encounter a failure mode where a widely repeated misconception becomes a highly probable continuation.

Core lesson: Language-model likelihood and factual truth are different concepts.

18. Deep Dive — SimpleQA

The outline describes SimpleQA through three ideas: open-ended short answers, calibration, and metrics.

Why Open-Ended Evaluation Is Harder

Suppose the reference answer is: 

Paris

The model might answer: 

The capital city of France is Paris.

A useful evaluator must recognize semantic correctness rather than blindly requiring exact string equality.

This makes evaluation design itself an important engineering problem.

21. Practical Evaluation Example

The following simplified Python example demonstrates the basic mechanics of a multiple-choice benchmark evaluator. It is intentionally framework-neutral so that the evaluation logic remains easy to understand. 

questions = [
    {
        "question": "What is the capital of France?",
        "choices": ["London", "Paris", "Berlin", "Madrid"],
        "answer": "Paris"
    },
    {
        "question": "What is 2 + 2?",
        "choices": ["3", "4", "5", "6"],
        "answer": "4"
    }
]

predictions = ["Paris", "4"]

correct = 0

for item, prediction in zip(questions, predictions):
    if prediction.strip().lower() == item["answer"].lower():
        correct += 1

accuracy = correct / len(questions)

print(f"Accuracy: {accuracy:.2%}")

Expected output:

Accuracy: 100.00%

Benchmark Evaluation Pipeline

Benchmark Dataset
       ↓
Prompt / Question
       ↓
LLM
       ↓
Prediction
       ↓
Evaluator
       ↓
Metric
       ↓
Aggregate Score
       ↓
Error Analysis
In production evaluation, do not stop at the aggregate score. Store individual predictions so that you can perform error analysis.

22. Key Takeaways

  • LLM capability is multidimensional. Knowledge, reasoning, truthfulness, calibration, and multimodal reasoning are different evaluation dimensions.
  • MMLU measures breadth. It is useful for understanding broad academic and professional knowledge.
  • TruthfulQA changes the question. Knowing information is different from consistently stating the truth.
  • Human examination benchmarks provide context. Human baselines can make model scores easier to interpret.
  • GPQA pushes toward depth. The focus moves toward difficult expert-level knowledge and reasoning.
  • MMLU-Pro addresses benchmark limitations. Harder questions and a more demanding evaluation setup can improve discrimination between capable models.
  • SimpleQA demonstrates the challenge of open-ended evaluation. Evaluators need to handle short answers, correctness, and calibration.
  • HLE represents a frontier-style direction. Breadth, depth, multimodality, and resistance to contamination become increasingly important.
  • Benchmark saturation is a real problem. A benchmark that becomes too easy stops being useful for distinguishing future models.
  • A benchmark score is not intelligence. It is evidence about performance on a particular evaluation instrument.

One-Line Mental Model

MMLU → breadth → TruthfulQA → truthfulness → AGI Eval → human exam comparison → GPQA → expert depth → MMLU-Pro → harder reasoning → SimpleQA → open-ended factuality/calibration → HLE → frontier breadth × depth.
